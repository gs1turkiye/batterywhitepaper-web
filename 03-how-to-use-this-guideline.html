3. How to use this guideline ​

This guideline is designed to be used as a decision path. First classify the battery, then identify every role performed by your organisation, determine the requirements that apply to that combination of battery category and role, and establish whether the battery requires a battery passport.

A battery's category alone does not determine all applicable obligations. The answer can also depend on characteristics such as rated capacity, whether the battery is incorporated in or supplied with another product, how and where it is supplied, and whether it has undergone preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing.

The Regulation requires particular functions and outcomes but does not prescribe the use of GS1 standards for the implementations described in this guideline. Where relevant, the guideline explains how GS1 standards may help implement those functions in an interoperable and globally unique manner. Their use does not by itself establish legal compliance.

3.1 Start with your battery category ​

Begin with Section 4, “Determining the battery category”. Determine whether the battery is:

  • a portable battery;
  • a portable battery of general use;
  • a light means of transport (LMT) battery;
  • a starting, lighting and ignition (SLI) battery;
  • an industrial battery; or
  • an electric vehicle (EV) battery.

The definitions and category boundaries are established in Article 3(1). A portable battery of general use is a defined subcategory of portable battery rather than an additional principal category. Other characteristics may also affect the applicable requirements. These include whether the battery is a button cell, whether it is incorporated in or supplied with another product, whether it forms part of a stationary battery energy storage system, its rated capacity and whether it has undergone a circular operation.

Where more than one classification initially appears possible, apply the definitions and category boundaries in Article 3(1). Do not determine battery-passport applicability until the category and, where relevant, rated capacity have been established.

After classification, consult the applicable category-specific section identified in Section 3.6.

3.2 Determine your economic-operator role ​

Article 3(1), point (22), defines an economic operator as a manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor, fulfilment service provider or another natural or legal person subject to specified obligations relating to batteries and their supply or use.

Roles are determined by the activities performed, not merely by the description an organisation uses for itself. The same organisation may perform more than one role. For example, an organisation may be both the manufacturer for product-conformity purposes and the producer for extended producer responsibility in a particular Member State. Identify every applicable role rather than selecting only one.

3.2.1 Manufacturer ​

You are generally the manufacturer if you manufacture a battery, have a battery designed or manufactured and market it under your own name or trademark, or put it into service for your own purposes. Manufacturer obligations include ensuring that the battery satisfies the applicable product requirements, completing the applicable conformity assessment, preparing technical documentation and the EU declaration of conformity, affixing the required CE marking and other markings, identifying the battery and taking corrective action where necessary (Articles 17–20 and 38).

An importer or distributor is treated as the manufacturer where it places a battery on the market under its own name or trademark or modifies a battery already placed on the market in a way that could affect compliance (Article 44).

See Section 11.1, “Manufacturer”, together with the relevant category-specific section.

3.2.2 Authorised representative ​

You are an authorised representative where you are established in the Union and have received a written mandate from a manufacturer to perform specified tasks. The mandate does not transfer all manufacturer obligations. In particular, the obligations laid down in Article 38(1) and the obligation to draw up technical documentation cannot form part of the authorised representative's mandate (Article 40(2)).

See Section 11.2, “Authorised representative”.

3.2.3 Importer ​

You are an importer if you are established in the Union and place on the Union market a battery from a third country. Before doing so, importers must verify matters including the applicable conformity assessment, technical documentation, EU declaration of conformity, CE marking, Article 13 marking and labelling, required documents and instructions, and manufacturer identification (Article 41(1)–(3)).

Importing a product containing a battery can also bring the incorporated battery within the importer's responsibilities under the Regulation.

See Section 11.3, “Importer”.

3.2.4 Distributor ​

You are a distributor if you make a battery available on the market but are not its manufacturer or importer. This role includes retailers and may include distance sellers. Distributor obligations include verifying specified information and conformity elements before making a battery available, protecting conformity during storage and transport, and taking action where a battery is non-compliant or presents a risk (Article 42).

Distributors also have specified waste-battery take-back and end-user information duties (Articles 62 and 74(4)).

See Section 11.4, “Distributor”.

3.2.5 Fulfilment service provider ​

A fulfilment service provider may perform activities such as warehousing, packaging, addressing and dispatching without owning the batteries concerned, subject to the definition incorporated from Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020. Article 43 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 requires fulfilment service providers to ensure that conditions under their responsibility do not jeopardise a battery's compliance and applies specified cooperation and risk-notification obligations.

See Section 11.5, “Fulfilment service provider”.

3.2.6 Producer for extended producer responsibility ​

“Producer” is a separate, Member-State-specific role defined in Article 3(1), point (47). It is not necessarily synonymous with “manufacturer”. Depending on how batteries are first supplied in a Member State, a manufacturer, importer, distributor, distance seller or another person can be the producer.

Producer obligations can include registration, financing and organising waste management, collection, reporting, and providing information to end-users and waste-management operators (Articles 55–58 and 59–75). Determine producer status separately for each Member State in which batteries are supplied. A GLN may complement official producer registration information, but it does not replace an authority-issued producer registration number.

See Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”.

3.2.7 Operator carrying out a circular operation ​

An economic operator that places on the market or puts into service a battery that has undergone preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing is considered to be the manufacturer for the purposes of the Regulation (Article 38(11)). Article 45 establishes additional quality-control, safety and compliance obligations for such economic operators.

Such an operator should not assume that the battery retains its previous category, conformity status or passport necessity. Classify the battery after the operation and determine the applicable conformity assessment, labelling, QR-code and battery-passport consequences.

See Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

3.2.8 Online-platform provider ​

An online-platform provider is not automatically the manufacturer, importer, distributor or producer. Article 62(6) nevertheless assigns specified responsibilities to providers of online platforms that allow consumers to conclude distance contracts with producers offering batteries, including batteries incorporated in appliances, light means of transport or other vehicles.

If an online-platform provider also imports, owns, sells or fulfils orders for the batteries, one or more additional roles may apply.

3.2.9 Waste-management operator ​

Collectors, treatment facilities, recyclers and operators preparing waste batteries for re-use or repurposing have obligations concerning matters such as handover, treatment, recycling, record keeping and reporting (Articles 65 and 70–75).

See Section 16, “Waste collection, treatment and recycling”, and Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”. EPCIS may support the capture and exchange of relevant visibility events, but EPCIS records do not by themselves demonstrate regulatory compliance or end-of-waste status.

3.2.10 End-user or battery holder ​

End-users must discard waste batteries separately from other waste streams at designated separate-collection points (Article 64). A battery holder seeking to demonstrate that a waste battery has ceased to be waste after preparation for re-use or preparation for repurposing may also need to provide the evidence specified in Article 73.

3.3 Determine the requirements for your battery type (category) and role ​

After determining the battery category and all roles performed, begin with the relevant category-specific section. That section explains the applicable regulatory requirements, responsible actors, battery passport necessity, labelling and QR-code obligations, key dates, exceptions and relevant GS1 implementation guidance.

For each potentially applicable requirement, determine:

  1. the battery category and any relevant subcategory, threshold, exclusion or derogation;
  2. the regulated actor;
  3. the required function, action or information;
  4. the application date;
  5. the required identification or data granularity;
  6. the required data carrier, document or reporting channel;
  7. whether and how a GS1 standard may support implementation; and
  8. any limitation, dependency or unresolved question.

Do not infer a requirement solely from its application to a similar battery category. For example, a requirement applying to an EV battery should not automatically be transferred to an industrial or portable battery. Similarly, do not apply a battery-passport requirement to a battery merely because the battery falls within the general scope of the Regulation.

3.4 Determine whether a battery passport is required ​

Record one of the following three outcomes for every battery:

OutcomeMeaning
Battery passport requiredFrom 18 February 2027, the battery falls within Article 77(1): an LMT battery, an industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh, or an EV battery.
Battery passport not required, but Article 13 QR code appliesThe battery is outside Article 77(1), but remains subject to the applicable QR-code requirements in Article 13(6)(b) or, for an SLI battery, Article 13(6)(b) and (c).
Further information requiredThe battery category or, for an industrial battery, its capacity has not been established sufficiently to determine whether Article 77(1) applies.

A physical QR code is not necessarily a battery passport. Assess the following concepts separately:

  • the physical QR-code requirement;
  • the information made accessible through the QR code;
  • the battery passport and its electronic record;
  • the passport unique identifier;
  • other physical labels and markings;
  • CE marking; and
  • voluntary web-enabled GS1 identification.

See Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”, for the common implementation detail.

A GTIN alone identifies a trade item or battery model; it does not identify an individual battery instance. A GLN does not replace a producer registration number or another identifier issued by an authority. A GS1 Digital Link URI can provide a web-enabled identification method, but it does not by itself create or satisfy the legal architecture of a battery passport. EPCIS can support the capture and sharing of events and associated evidence, but it does not replace verification, certification, audit or an authority decision.

For the detailed implementation methods and worked examples, see Sections 12, 13, 18, 19 and 20.

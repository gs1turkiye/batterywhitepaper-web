12. Battery and grouping identification ​

12.1 Regulatory identification functions ​

The Regulation requires batteries to be identified, but the required level of identification depends on the purpose it serves and the information it enables access to. Manufacturers must ensure that batteries carry appropriate identification to meet the relevant regulatory requirements. Depending on the use case, this identification may be needed at model, batch and/or individual battery level. It may need to be placed directly on the battery, or, where the battery’s size or nature makes this impractical, on the packaging or accompanying documentation. In some cases, the Regulation also requires the battery to be distinguished from the product or equipment in which it is installed, meaning that both the battery and the host product may need separate identifiers. GS1 standards provide a flexible framework to support all of these identification needs (Articles 38(6), 41(2)(d) and 42(2)(d)).

12.2 Battery as a trade item ​

A battery is a trade item when it is a product for which predefined information needs to be retrieved and that may be priced, ordered or invoiced at a point in the supply chain.

The trade item may be a cell, module, battery pack or another battery configuration. Where a battery pack and a replaceable module are each traded separately, each distinct trade item should be allocated its own GTIN.

GTIN identifies the trade-item at model level.

12.3 Battery incorporated in another trade item ​

The Regulation applies to batteries incorporated into products and requires identification of the battery itself. Relevant removability, replaceability and information duties remain subject to their express scope and derogations (Articles 1(3), 11 and 38(6)).

The battery and host product are separate identification and data objects. Where each is a trade item, the battery GTIN identifies the battery and the host-product GTIN identifies the appliance, equipment or vehicle. Battery attributes must describe the battery, not the host product.

Their relationship should be maintained at the granularity needed for the function. Model-to-model association may suffice for an unchanged design. Installation, recall, service, replacement or passport access may require batch or instance relationship.

12.4 Battery supplied with another trade item ​

A battery supplied with, but not incorporated into, another product remains a separate object. Battery and host product may each have a GTIN, while a combined retail or supply configuration may require its own GTIN under the applicable allocation rules.

The implementation must state whether each attribute describes the battery, host product or combined configuration. An outer trade-item identifier does not identify each contained battery instance; that requires individual identifiers and a recorded containment relationship.

12.5 Logistic-unit identification using SSCC ​

SSCC identifies a logistic unit such as a pallet, case, cage or container assembled for transport and/or storage. It supports shipping, receiving, custody and aggregation records.

The content relationship must associate the SSCC with contained trade items and any recorded batch or serial identifiers. When a logistic unit is reconfigured, identification and content records must follow the applicable SSCC rules.

SSCC does not identify a battery model, batch or instance and cannot serve as the unique identifier for the trade item.

12.6 Choosing the appropriate identification level ​

Product identification, identification of product grouping and logistic unit identification serve different functions.

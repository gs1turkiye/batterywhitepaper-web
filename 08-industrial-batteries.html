8. Industrial batteries ​

8.1 Battery passport necessity ​

The capacity threshold determines the passport outcome for industrial batteries within the temporal scope of Article 77(1):

Industrial-battery capacityBattery-passport outcome
Greater than 2 kWhBattery passport required
Exactly 2 kWh or lessNo battery passport under Article 77(1); the Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route applies
Capacity not establishedFurther capacity information is required before passport necessity can be determined

See Section 3.4, “Determine whether a battery passport is required”, and Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

8.2 Product, sustainability and safety requirements ​

The following requirements have a specific or distinct application to industrial batteries:

RequirementEffect on industrial batteriesProvision
Carbon footprintFor rechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh, a declaration for each battery model per manufacturing plant; at later conditional dates, a carbon-footprint performance-class label and a maximum life-cycle carbon-footprint threshold. Different dates apply to batteries with exclusively external storage.Article 7(1)–(3); Annex II
Recycled contentFor industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh other than those with exclusively external storage, documentation of the percentages of cobalt, lead, lithium and nickel recovered from waste and present in active materials; at later dates, minimum recovered-content sharesArticle 8(1)–(3)
Performance and durabilityFor rechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh, a document accompanying the battery with the applicable electrochemical performance and durability parameter values and technical documentation explaining how those values were determined; at a later conditional date, minimum values that exclude batteries with exclusively external storageArticle 10(1)–(2); Annex IV, Part A
Stationary storage safetyFor stationary battery energy storage systems, technical documentation demonstrating compliance with the applicable safety parameters and addressing identified hazards through testing, mitigation measures and instructionsArticle 12; Annex V
State of health and expected lifetimeFor stationary battery energy storage systems, current state-of-health and expected-lifetime data in the battery management system; read-only access for specified purposes; software reset functionality for circular operationsArticle 14; Annex VII

Article 10(1) has applied since 18 August 2024 to rechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh. It requires the battery to be accompanied by the Annex IV, Part A performance and durability values and requires the technical documentation to explain how they were measured, calculated or estimated. Minimum values under Article 10(2) apply only from its conditional date and exclude rechargeable industrial batteries with exclusively external storage.

Article 12(1) requires stationary battery energy storage systems to be safe during normal operation and use. By 18 August 2024, the technical documentation had to demonstrate compliance with the applicable Annex V safety parameters and address other identified hazards (Article 12(2)). Article 14 has required the specified battery-management-system data and access since 18 August 2024. These duties apply according to stationary-system status and are not displaced by the 2 kWh passport threshold.

Requirements common to several battery categories—including restricted substances, conformity assessment, CE marking, manufacturer, importer and distributor duties, and market surveillance—are addressed in Sections 10 and 11.

8.3 Carbon footprint and recycled content ​

Article 7 establishes three conditional stages for rechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh:

StageRechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh, other than batteries with exclusively external storageRechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh with exclusively external storageProvision
Carbon-footprint declaration18 February 2026 or 18 months after the relevant acts enter into force, whichever is later18 August 2030 or 18 months after the relevant acts enter into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(1)
Carbon-footprint value and performance-class label18 August 2027 or 18 months after the relevant acts enter into force, whichever is later18 February 2032 or 18 months after the relevant acts enter into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(2)
Maximum life-cycle carbon-footprint threshold18 February 2029 or 18 months after the relevant act enters into force, whichever is later18 August 2033 or 18 months after the relevant act enters into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(3)

The declaration is prepared for each battery model per manufacturing plant. Until it is accessible through the QR code, it accompanies the battery.

For industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh other than those with exclusively external storage, Article 8 requires recovered-material documentation where the relevant materials are present (Article 8(1)–(3)). It applies from 18 August 2028 or 24 months after the Article 8(1) delegated act enters into force, whichever is later. Minimum recovered-content shares apply from 18 August 2031 and increase from 18 August 2036.

The Article 7 and 8 exemptions for batteries already placed on the market or put into service before a listed circular operation are preserved in Articles 7(5) and 8(4). Applicable delegated and implementing acts must be checked before a conditional date, methodology, format or value is treated as operative.

See Section 10, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”.

8.4 Labelling, QR code and passport information ​

The QR-code route depends on capacity from 18 February 2027:

  • for an industrial battery greater than 2 kWh, the QR code provides access to the battery passport under Article 13(6)(a) and Article 77(1); and
  • for an industrial battery of exactly 2 kWh or less, the QR code provides access to the information specified in Article 13(6)(b).

For rechargeable industrial batteries greater than 2 kWh, the carbon-footprint label applies on the relevant conditional date in Article 7(2). Other Article 13 labels and markings apply according to their common scope and dates.

See Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”, for the common passport content, access and governance requirements, and Section 11, “Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance”, for the responsible actors.

8.5 Due diligence, producer responsibility and end of life ​

Battery due diligence is not determined by the 2 kWh passport threshold. It applies subject to the economic-operator threshold and exclusions in Article 47 and the requirements in Articles 48–52. The current application date must be verified against Regulation (EU) 2025/1561; the controlled base Regulation predates that amendment. See Section 14, “Battery due diligence”.

A producer must register in each Member State in which it first makes an industrial battery available and may not supply there unless registered (Article 55(1), (2) and (12)). Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must accept all waste industrial batteries of the category they made available, free of charge and without requiring a new purchase (Article 61(1)). Article 61 does not establish a percentage collection target for industrial batteries. Annual category-and-chemistry reporting applies under Article 75(2). See Sections 15 and 16.

After a listed circular operation, the operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is treated as the manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). An industrial battery greater than 2 kWh within Article 77(1) requires a new passport linked to the original passport or passports under Article 77(7). Article 73 governs the evidence required to demonstrate that a waste industrial battery prepared for re-use or preparation for repurposing has ceased to be waste. See Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

8.8 Limitations and open questions ​

The definitions and distinctions in Section 22, “Glossary”, apply. The 2 kWh threshold determines passport applicability but does not determine every other industrial-battery obligation. Exclusively external-storage batteries and batteries subjected to circular operations retain the specific qualifications in Articles 7, 8 and 10.

Current-law checks remain necessary for capacity evidence at the passport boundary (OQ-REG-005), circular-operation QR information outside passport scope (OQ-REG-007), the due-diligence application date (OQ-REG-009), reporting implementation (OQ-REG-011), end-of-waste technical requirements (OQ-REG-012), the GS1 Digital Link governance model (OQ-GS1-003) and GDSN data coverage (OQ-GS1-001). GS1 identifiers and EPCIS support interoperability but do not establish legal compliance.

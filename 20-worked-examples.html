20. Worked examples ​

These examples show how the classification, regulatory and GS1 implementation decisions in the preceding sections can be applied. They are illustrative, not complete compliance checklists. Each example states assumed facts; a change in intended use, capacity, configuration, actor or date can change the result. The Regulation does not prescribe GTIN, GLN, SSCC, GS1 Digital Link or EPCIS for these examples.

In the conceptual identifiers below, example.com and the numbers are placeholders, not production identifiers or allocation advice. The technical roles described are supported by the GS1 General Specifications, GS1 Digital Link URI Syntax, GS1 GLN Allocation Rules, GTIN Management Standard and EPCIS and CBV standards in the controlled source set. See Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”, Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”, and Section 18, “GS1 implementation models”.

20.1 General-use portable battery ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places a retail pack of four rechargeable AA batteries on the Union market after 18 February 2027. The batteries are specifically designed to be interoperable and are portable batteries of general use under Article 3(1), point (10). Each cell is the same model and production batch; the pack is the orderable retail trade item.

Regulatory outcome. The batteries do not require passports under Article 77(1), but each battery is subject to the Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route. General and capacity labelling must be determined under Article 13(1)–(2), Annex VI and the conditional application rule. Separate-collection and, where the threshold is exceeded, chemical-symbol requirements also apply (Article 13(4)–(5)). Minimum performance and durability values apply subject to Article 9, Annex III and the applicable implementing act; button cells are excluded (Article 9(1)–(2)).

GS1 implementation. The manufacturer may allocate a GTIN to the retail pack and, if the cells are separately traded or require their own information association, a GTIN to the cell trade item. GTIN plus batch/lot number can associate the cells with batch-specific records. A GS1 Digital Link URI encoded in the required QR code may provide a web entry point to the applicable Article 13(6)(b) resources, for example https://example.com/01/{GTIN}/10/{BATCH}.

This is a non-passport information service. A pack GTIN does not identify each cell, and Article 13(7) must be assessed before relying on packaging and accompanying documents. Serialisation is not required merely because a QR code is required. OQ-REG-001 and OQ-REG-003 remain relevant.

20.2 Portable rechargeable battery incorporated in an appliance ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places on the market a cordless appliance containing a rechargeable portable battery. The appliance and battery are distinct trade items. The product is not within an Article 11(2) derogation or Article 11(3) exclusion.

Regulatory outcome. The battery does not require a passport under Article 77(1), but Article 13(6)(b) applies from 18 February 2027. From that date, the person placing the product on the market must ensure that the battery is readily removable and replaceable by the end-user and provide the required instructions and safety information, subject to Article 11(1)–(3) and (7)–(8). The manufacturer must identify the battery under Article 38(6). Article 13(7) governs alternative QR and label placement.

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the appliance trade item and a separate GTIN identifies the battery trade item. A model-to-model association may support product information; installation, service, safety investigation or recall may require a batch- or instance-level relationship. Battery attributes must describe the battery, not the appliance. A battery GS1 Digital Link URI may connect to the Article 13(6)(b) information. The appliance identifier must not be used as the battery identifier. Article 11 derogations and exclusions must be preserved; OQ-REG-004 remains relevant.

20.3 LMT battery ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places an individually serialised traction battery for an electric bicycle on the market on or after 18 February 2027. It is an LMT battery under Article 3(1), point (11).

Regulatory outcome. Each LMT battery requires an individual passport; no capacity threshold applies (Article 77(1)). Its QR code provides access to the passport (Article 13(6)(a)). The operator placing the battery on the market attributes the unique identifier, and the QR code and identifier must comply with Article 77(3). Passport content and access are governed by Articles 77–78 and Annex XIII. LMT-specific duties also include applicable carbon-footprint stages (Article 7), performance and durability requirements (Article 10(2)–(3); Annex IV), and state-of-health and expected-lifetime data in the BMS (Article 14; Annex VII).

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the battery trade item; GTIN plus serial number identifies the instance. A conceptual GS1 Digital Link URI is https://example.com/01/{GTIN}/21/{SERIAL}. It may provide the web-enabled identifier through which the passport is accessed. The operator may use GLN; GLN identifies a party or plant. EPCIS may capture lifecycle events.

The complete implementation must satisfy Article 77(3)–(6); GS1 use alone does not establish that result. The resolver, BMS and EPCIS repository are not the passport. The host product remains a separately identified object.

20.4 SLI battery ​

Assumed facts. A producer places a 12-volt rechargeable starting battery for a conventional vehicle on the market. It is an SLI battery under Article 3(1), point (12), not an EV battery.

Regulatory outcome. It does not require a passport under Article 77(1). From 18 February 2027, Article 13(6)(b) applies and its QR code must also provide access to applicable recycled-content information under Article 13(6)(c). Recycled-content duties follow Article 8. The capacity label applies under Article 13(2). Waste SLI batteries must be accepted free of charge without requiring a new purchase (Article 61(1)).

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the trade item. GTIN plus batch/lot number may support quality records and recall; GTIN plus serial number may support warranty or fleet servicing. A GS1 Digital Link URI may connect to the non-passport information. SSCC may identify a pallet, with its contents associated separately.

Serialisation is not an Article 77 duty here. SSCC does not identify contained batteries, and GLN does not replace a national producer registration number. OQ-REG-006 remains relevant to the timing of Article 13(6)(c) information.

20.5 Industrial battery not exceeding 2 kWh ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places a 2 kWh rechargeable battery on the market solely for industrial use. It is not an LMT, EV or SLI battery or a stationary battery energy storage system. Capacity is evidenced on the applicable technical basis.

Regulatory outcome. Article 77(1) applies to an industrial battery only above 2 kWh, so a battery at exactly 2 kWh does not require a passport. Article 13(6)(b) applies from 18 February 2027. Industrial-battery waste take-back applies irrespective of the passport threshold (Article 61(1)–(4)). Due-diligence scope depends on Article 47, not passport status.

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the trade item. GTIN plus batch/lot number may connect production groups to test evidence; GTIN plus serial number may support warranty or servicing. A GS1 Digital Link URI may connect the chosen identifier to non-passport information. The operator may use GLN; GLN identifies parties or sites. SSCC identifies a shipment unit.

GS1 granularity cannot determine legal capacity or category, and a serialised information page is not a passport. GDSN use requires verified field and target-market coverage; OQ-GS1-001 remains open.

20.6 Industrial battery exceeding 2 kWh ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places a separately traded, serialised 5 kWh industrial battery on the market after 18 February 2027.

Regulatory outcome. Each battery requires a passport because its capacity is greater than 2 kWh (Article 77(1)); Article 13(6)(a) provides the QR route. Performance and durability documentation applies under Article 10(1) and Annex IV, Part A. Carbon-footprint and recycled-content duties apply only within their stated category, capacity, date and delegated-act conditions (Articles 7–8).

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the trade item and GTIN plus serial number identifies each passport-bearing instance. A GS1 Digital Link URI can connect that identifier to the passport. Model data may be associated with the GTIN and instance data with the serialised identifier. EPCIS may capture shipment, installation, maintenance and circular events. The operator may use GLN; GLN identifies parties and locations.

GTIN alone is insufficient for an individual passport. Identifiers must not be reassigned when a website or service provider changes. The responsible operator retains the Article 77(4) data-governance duties; GS1 identifiers and EPCIS do not replace them.

20.7 Stationary battery energy storage system ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places a 100 kWh rechargeable stationary battery energy storage system on the market for an industrial installation. It meets Article 3(1), point (15).

Regulatory outcome. The system is an industrial battery and, because it exceeds 2 kWh, each battery within Article 77(1) requires a passport from 18 February 2027. Article 12 requires technical documentation and evidence of successful safety testing against Annex V (Article 12(1)–(2)). Article 14 and Annex VII govern BMS state-of-health and expected-lifetime data.

GS1 implementation. The operator first defines the passport-bearing battery and distinguishes it from modules, racks, installation and host equipment. GTIN plus serial number may identify that battery. Separate GTINs may identify independently traded modules, and recorded relationships can support replacement. GLNs identify parties or locations; EPCIS may record commissioning and module replacement.

Internal module tracking does not itself create a passport duty for every module. The legal and technical battery boundary must be established, and component, system and location identifiers are not interchangeable. OQ-REG-005 remains relevant to stationary systems at or below 2 kWh and capacity evidence.

20.8 Electric vehicle battery ​

Assumed facts. A manufacturer places a traction battery for a battery electric passenger vehicle on the market on or after 18 February 2027. It is an EV battery under Article 3(1), point (14), installed in an identified vehicle.

Regulatory outcome. Each EV battery requires a passport without a capacity threshold (Article 77(1)), accessed through Article 13(6)(a). Annex XIII and Articles 77–78 govern information, access, update, interoperability and persistence. Applicable carbon-footprint, recycled-content, performance-documentation and BMS duties arise under Articles 7, 8, 10(1) and 14.

GS1 implementation. GTIN identifies the battery trade item; GTIN plus serial number identifies the passport-bearing instance. The vehicle and battery retain separate identifiers with an explicit installation relationship. A GS1 Digital Link URI may provide the web entry point, while EPCIS may capture manufacture, shipment, installation, removal and transfer events.

The passport, vehicle record, BMS and EPCIS repository remain separate systems. Article 77–78 access controls apply, and event records do not prove conformity. Conditional Article 7 and 8 dates require verification against the relevant acts.

20.9 Repurposed battery ​

Assumed facts. An operator combines modules from two former EV batteries into a 12 kWh battery for stationary industrial energy storage and places it on the market after 18 February 2027. The operation is repurposing, the result is a battery, and applicable end-of-waste and conformity conditions have been satisfied.

Regulatory outcome. The result is an industrial battery and, because it exceeds 2 kWh, requires a new passport (Article 3(1), points (18) and (26); Article 77(1), (7)). The operator is treated as manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). Article 13(9) requires new labels or markings and QR-accessible status information. The new passport must link to both original passports, and responsibility transfers as specified in Article 77(7).

GS1 implementation. Applying the GTIN Management Standard, the operator may allocate a GTIN to the resulting trade item and combine it with a new serial number. Original and resulting identifiers remain distinct. EPCIS may support many-to-one lineage, and GLNs may identify the operator and facility. The new GS1 Digital Link URI may access the new passport; links to original passports must persist.

An original serialised identifier must not be reused for the different resulting battery. EPCIS does not establish end-of-waste status, conformity or classification. If the result were exactly 2 kWh or less, no Article 77 passport would apply, although Article 13(9) would; OQ-REG-007 and OQ-REG-012 remain relevant.

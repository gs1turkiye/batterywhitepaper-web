6. Light means of transport batteries ​

6.1 Battery passport necessity ​

Every LMT battery within the temporal scope of Article 77(1) requires a battery passport; no capacity threshold applies to this category.

6.2 Product and sustainability requirements ​

RequirementEffect on LMT batteriesProvision
Carbon footprintModel-per-plant declaration; later performance-class label and maximum thresholdArticle 7(1)–(3); Annex II
Recycled contentDocumentation date and increasing minimum shares under the RegulationArticle 8(1) and (3)
Performance and durabilityValues and technical explanation; at a later date minimum values will be definedArticle 10(1), (3) and (5); Annex IV
Removability and replaceabilityBattery and cells removable and replaceable by an independent professional; the battery must remain available as spare-part and software must not impede replacementArticle 11(5)–(8)
State of health and expected lifetimeCurrent state-of-health and expected-lifetime data in the battery management system; read-only access for specified purposes; software reset for circular operationsArticle 14; Annex VII

Article 10(1) has applied since 18 August 2024. The LMT battery must be accompanied by the stated performance and durability values, and the technical documentation must explain how they were measured, calculated or estimated. Minimum values apply from the conditional date in Article 10(3), based on the delegated act specified in Article 10(5).

Article 14 has applied since 18 August 2024. The person who legally purchased the battery, including independent operators and waste management operators, or a person acting on that purchaser’s behalf, is to receive read-only, non-discriminatory access to the battery-management-system data for the purposes specified in Article 14(1). Annex VII specifies LMT state-of-health and expected-lifetime parameters. The battery management system and battery passport are related information sources but are not the same system.

From 18 February 2027, the person placing a product incorporating an LMT battery on the market must ensure that the battery and the individual cells in its pack are readily removable and replaceable by an independent professional throughout the product lifetime (Article 11(5)–(8); Article 96(2)(a)). Compatibility must preserve the functioning, performance and safety of the light means of transport. The battery must be available as a spare part for at least five years after the last unit of the equipment model is placed on the market, at a reasonable and non-discriminatory price, and software must not impede replacement with a compatible battery or key components.

Common requirements, including restricted substances, conformity assessment, CE marking, economic-operator duties and market surveillance, are addressed in Sections 10 and 11.

The declaration is prepared for each battery model per manufacturing plant. Until accessible through the QR code, it accompanies the battery.

For LMT batteries containing the relevant materials, recovered-material documentation applies from 18 August 2033; minimum recovered-content shares apply from 18 August 2036, while the 2031 shares do not apply to LMT batteries (Article 8(1)–(3)).

The circular-operation exemptions in Articles 7(5) and 8(4) remain applicable. Relevant delegated and implementing acts must be checked before a conditional date, methodology, format or value is treated as operative. See Section 10, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”.

6.4 Labelling, QR code and passport information ​

In addition to common Article 13 markings, the carbon-footprint label applies on the conditional date in Article 7(2), and the QR code provides access to the battery passport from 18 February 2027 (Article 13(6)(a)).

The LMT passport includes the applicable model-level and individual-battery information in Annex XIII. This includes Article 10 performance and durability values, Article 14 state-of-health information, battery status and specified information resulting from use (Annex XIII, point 4). The capacity threshold for exhaustion in Annex XIII, point 1(k) is expressly limited to EV batteries and is not an LMT-battery field.

See Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”, for the common passport content, access and governance requirements, and Section 11, “Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance”, for the responsible actors.

6.5 Due diligence, producer responsibility and end of life ​

Battery due diligence is not triggered by passport scope alone. It applies subject to the economic-operator threshold and exclusions in Article 47 and the requirements in Articles 48–52. The current application date must be verified against Regulation (EU) 2025/1561; the controlled base Regulation predates that amendment. See Section 14, “Battery due diligence”.

A producer must register in each Member State in which it first makes an LMT battery available and may not supply there unless registered (Article 55(1), (2) and (12)). Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must establish a territory-wide take-back and collection system and attain and durably maintain collection targets of 51% by 31 December 2028 and 61% by 31 December 2031 (Article 60(1)–(3)). End-users must be able to discard waste LMT batteries at connected collection points without charge, without buying a new battery and without having bought the battery from the producer operating the point (Article 60(5)). Annual category-and-chemistry reporting applies under Article 75(2). See Sections 15 and 16.

After a listed circular operation, the operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is treated as the manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). A new passport linked to the original passport or passports is required under Article 77(7). A battery repurposed for a different use must be classified according to its new intended use; this may change the applicable requirements and passport outcome. See Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

6.6 GS1 implementation for LMT batteries ​

The Regulation requires identification and information-access functions but does not prescribe GS1 standards.

FunctionHow GS1 standards may support itGranularity and limitation
Identify the model and passport instanceAn implementation may use GTIN and serialisation. GTIN identifies the model or trade item; GTIN plus serial number identifies the individual batteryGTIN alone is not an individual identifier. The identifier and QR implementation must satisfy Article 77(3).
Maintain the battery–LMT relationshipSeparate identifiers for the battery and light means of transport may be associated in business recordsHost-product identification does not identify the battery instance; the relationship must be explicit.
Provide web-enabled accessA GS1 Digital Link URI may express the GTIN and serial number and connect the battery to controlled resourcesThe URI and resolver are not the passport. Article 77–78 requirements remain applicable.
Identify organisations and sitesAn implementation may use GLNs. GLNs identify parties, functions and locationsA GLN does not replace an authority-issued registration or other legal identifier.
Identify logistic unitsAn implementation may use SSCC. SSCC identifies a logistic unit containing batteriesThe SSCC does not identify the contained battery instances; their relationship must be recorded.
Share lifecycle visibility dataAn implementation may use EPCIS. EPCIS captures and shares events concerning batteries, logistic units, parties and locationsEPCIS does not prove compliance or replace the passport, conformity assessment, verification, certification, audit or an authority decision.

Because each LMT battery requires an individual passport, the implementation must support instance-level association. A GS1 implementation will normally use GTIN plus serial number for the battery instance, while retaining model and, where relevant, batch or lot relationships. Where incorporated in or supplied with a light means of transport, the battery and host product remain separately identified objects.

See Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”; Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”; and Section 19, “Supply-chain traceability and information exchange”.

6.8 Limitations and open questions ​

The definitions and distinctions in Section 22, “Glossary”, apply. GS1 identifiers and EPCIS support interoperability but do not establish legal compliance.

Current-law checks remain necessary for Article 11 guidance and any adopted derogations (OQ-REG-004), the due-diligence application date (OQ-REG-009), reporting implementation (OQ-REG-011), collection-rate methodology or target amendments (OQ-REG-013), passport access measures, the GS1 Digital Link governance model (OQ-GS1-003) and GDSN data coverage (OQ-GS1-001).

22. Glossary ​

This glossary centralises definitions used across the guideline. Category-specific sections state the applicable result and refer here rather than repeating these concepts.

22.1 Regulatory terms ​

TermMeaning in this guidelineSource and qualification
Battery passportThe electronic record required from 18 February 2027 for each LMT battery, each industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh and each electric vehicle battery placed on the market or put into service. It contains battery-model information and information specific to the individual battery. It is accessible through the Article 13(6) QR code, which links to the unique identifier attributed by the economic operator placing the battery on the market. The QR code and unique identifier must comply with the ISO/IEC 15459 standards listed in Article 77(3), or equivalent. The Regulation does not prescribe a GS1 identifier. Article 77(1) applies by reference to placement on the market or putting into service from 18 February 2027; it does not state that a battery placed on the market or put into service before that date acquires a passport merely by remaining in service. Following preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing, a battery placed on the market or put into service is to have a new passport linked to the passport or passports of the original battery or batteries, and responsibility transfers to the operator specified in Article 77(7).Articles 13(6), 77(1)–(7) and 78; Annex XIII. The treatment of batteries placed on the market or put into service before 18 February 2027 follows the temporal wording of Article 77(1); later placement or putting into service after a circular operation must be assessed under Articles 38(11), 45 and 77(7).
Battery-passport unique identifierThe unique string of characters that identifies a battery and also enables a web link to its battery passport. For a passport battery, the economic operator placing the battery on the market attributes the identifier, and the QR code links to it.Article 3(1), point (66); Article 77(3). The term concerns batteries within Article 77(1) and must not be applied automatically to the QR-code arrangements for other batteries. A GS1 identification structure may support this function only where the complete implementation meets the Regulation’s requirements.
QR codeA machine-readable matrix code that links to information required by the Regulation. For a passport battery, the QR code provides access to the battery passport; for batteries outside Article 77(1), Article 13(6)(b) specifies a different information set.Article 3(1), point (24); Article 13(6). A QR code is not, by itself, a battery passport.
Placing on the marketThe first making available of a battery on the Union market.Article 3(1), point (16).
Putting into serviceThe first use, for its intended purpose in the Union, of a battery that has not previously been placed on the market.Article 3(1), point (18).

22.2 Battery-category terms ​

These concise definitions support consistent terminology. Section 4, “Determining the battery category”, explains the classification sequence, boundaries and borderline cases.

TermMeaning in this guidelineSource and qualification
Electric vehicle batteryA battery specifically designed to provide traction power in a hybrid or electric category-L vehicle that weighs more than 25 kg, or a battery specifically designed to provide traction power in a hybrid or electric vehicle of category M, N or O.Article 3(1), point (14). The referenced vehicle categories are those in Regulations (EU) No 168/2013 and (EU) 2018/858.
Industrial batteryA battery specifically designed for industrial uses; a battery intended for industrial uses after preparation for repurposing or repurposing; or any other battery weighing more than 5 kg that is not an electric vehicle, LMT or SLI battery.Article 3(1), point (13). The Article 77(1) passport threshold is capacity greater than 2 kWh, not battery weight.
Light means of transport battery (LMT battery)A sealed battery weighing 25 kg or less, specifically designed to provide traction power for wheeled vehicles powered by an electric motor alone or by motor and human power, including type-approved category-L vehicles, and which is not an electric vehicle battery.Article 3(1), point (11). No capacity threshold limits the Article 77(1) passport duty for an LMT battery.
Portable batteryA sealed battery weighing 5 kg or less, not designed specifically for industrial use, that is not an electric vehicle, LMT or SLI battery.Article 3(1), point (9). “Portable” is a defined category and is not determined by weight alone.
Portable battery of general useA rechargeable or non-rechargeable portable battery specifically designed to be interoperable and having one of these formats: 4.5 V (3R12), button cell, D, C, AA, AAA, AAAA, A23 or 9 V (PP3).Article 3(1), point (10). This is a subcategory of portable battery.
Starting, lighting and ignition battery (SLI battery)A battery specifically designed to supply electric power for starting, lighting or ignition and which can also be used for auxiliary or backup purposes in vehicles, other means of transport or machinery.Article 3(1), point (12).
Stationary battery energy storage systemAn industrial battery with internal storage, specifically designed to store electric energy from and deliver it to the grid, or to store electric energy for and deliver it to end-users, regardless of where and by whom it is used.Article 3(1), point (15). It remains an industrial battery; its passport status depends on whether its capacity is greater than 2 kWh (Article 77(1)).

22.3 Economic-operator terms ​

The roles are not interchangeable. In particular, “producer” is the role used for extended producer responsibility and is determined Member State by Member State under Article 3(1), point (47). See Section 11, “Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance”, and Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”, for the applicable duties.

TermMeaning in this guidelineSource and qualification
Authorised representativeA person established in the Union that has a written mandate from a manufacturer to act on its behalf for specified tasks concerning the manufacturer’s obligations under Chapters IV and VI.Article 3(1), point (63). This role is distinct from an authorised representative for extended producer responsibility under Article 3(1), point (48).
DistributorA person in the supply chain, other than the manufacturer or importer, that makes a battery available on the market.Article 3(1), point (65).
Economic operatorA manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor, fulfilment service provider or another person subject to obligations concerning the manufacture or circular processing of batteries, their making available or placing on the market, including online, or their putting into service.Article 3(1), point (22). The actor responsible for a particular duty must be determined from the operative provision; the general definition does not assign every duty to every economic operator.
ImporterA person established in the Union that places on the market a battery from a third country.Article 3(1), point (64).
ManufacturerA person that manufactures a battery, or has a battery designed or manufactured, and markets it under its own name or trademark or puts it into service for its own purposes.Article 3(1), point (33). A manufacturer is not necessarily the “producer” for every Member State.
ProducerA manufacturer, importer, distributor or other person that meets one of the Member-State-specific first-supply or distance-selling conditions in Article 3(1), point (47), including for batteries incorporated in appliances, light means of transport or other vehicles.Article 3(1), point (47). This concise definition must be applied with all four alternatives in points (a)–(d); it is not a synonym for manufacturer.

22.4 GS1 terminology ​

TermMeaning in this guidelineOfficial GS1 source and qualification
GTINGS1 identification key that identifies a trade item. A GTIN alone does not identify an individual battery instance.GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1, p. 35. Whether the battery, a host product or both are trade items requiring separate identification depends on the implementation scope.
GTIN plus batch/lot numberGS1 identification structure that distinguishes a production batch/lot of a trade item.GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1.2, p. 37. Batch-level identification does not identify an individual instance.
GTIN plus serial numberGS1 identification structure that distinguishes an individual instance of a trade item.GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1.4, p. 39. Allocation and lifecycle governance remain necessary.
GLNGS1 identification key that identifies a legal entity, party, function or location.GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.3–1.3.4, pp. 49–54. A GLN does not replace an authority-issued registration number or other legally issued identifier, but may complement and link to it.
SSCCGS1 identification key that identifies a logistic unit.GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.5, pp. 55–56. It does not identify each battery contained in the logistic unit.
GS1 Application IdentifierA numeric code that defines the meaning and format of the data element that follows it in a GS1 element string.GS1 General Specifications, section 3, pp. 201–276. Relevant examples include AI (01) for GTIN, AI (10) for batch/lot number and AI (21) for serial number.
GS1 Digital Link URIA URI representation of a GS1 identifier and, where applicable, its key qualifiers and data attributes. It can provide web-enabled identification and an entry point to online resources associated with the identified object.GS1 Digital Link Standard: URI Syntax, sections 3.1 and 4, pp. 18–36. A URI, resolver or linked webpage is not by itself a battery passport and does not satisfy the passport’s data, access, persistence, security or governance requirements.
EPCISGS1 standard for capturing and sharing visibility event data concerning identified objects and business processes.GS1 EPCIS Standard, version 2.0.1, sections 1, 6 and 7, pp. 21–102. EPCIS records do not prove regulatory compliance or replace conformity assessment, verification, certification, audit or an authority decision.
GDSNGS1 network for standardised trade-item master-data exchange.Use is subject to verification that current attributes, validations and target-market implementation cover the intended battery data. GDSN must not be presented as covering every regulatory data element until that mapping has been verified; see OQ-GS1-001.

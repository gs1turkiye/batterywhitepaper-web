16. Waste collection, treatment and recycling ​

This section applies to waste batteries in every battery category, whether or not the battery was subject to a mandatory battery passport. Chapter VIII establishes category-specific collection arrangements and common treatment, recycling and reporting requirements. It does not prescribe GS1 identifiers or EPCIS; these standards may support interoperable records but do not establish legal compliance.

16.1 Category-specific collection systems ​

Producers, or producer responsibility organisations where appointed, are responsible for arrangements covering batteries regardless of nature, chemistry, condition, brand or origin within the category.

CategoryRequired outcomeLegal basis
Portable, including general-useFree, territory-wide separate collection through connected points and delivery to a permitted treatment facility.Article 59(1), (2), (4)–(6)
LMTFree, territory-wide separate collection with suitable infrastructure and delivery to permitted facilities.Article 60(1), (2), (4)–(7)
SLIFree take-back and separate collection throughout the territory.Article 61(1)–(4)
Industrial, including stationary battery energy storage systemsThe Article 61 duty applies irrespective of capacity. Private, non-commercial users must not bear dismantling and collection costs where prior dismantling is required at their premises.Article 61(1)–(4)
Electric vehicleFree take-back and separate collection throughout the territory.Article 61(1)–(4)

Distributors must take back qualifying waste batteries free of charge without requiring a purchase. The duty is limited to categories that the distributor has or had in its offer and excludes waste products containing batteries. Distance sellers must provide sufficient collection points; delivery sales require a free-return offer and information when ordering (Article 62(1)–(5)). End-users must discard waste batteries separately from other waste, including mixed municipal waste (Article 64(1) and (2)).

16.2 Collection targets ​

CategoryMinimum collection rateBasis
Portable45% by 31 December 2023; 63% by 31 December 2027; 73% by 31 December 2030Article 59(3); Annex XI
LMT51% by 31 December 2028; 61% by 31 December 2031Article 60(3); Annex XI

Article 61 sets no percentage target for SLI, industrial or EV batteries. Annex XI divides weight collected in a year by average annual weight first made available to end-users during the preceding three years, calculated separately for portable and LMT batteries (Annex XI, points 1–4). The Commission may amend the methods and targets (Article 59(7); Article 60(8)); current acts must be checked before configuring calculations.

16.3 End-user information ​

Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must provide information on waste prevention and extending use; separate collection; available collection, preparation and treatment routes; safe handling, including lithium risks; label meanings; and impacts of substances and inappropriate disposal (Article 74(1)(a)–(f), (4) and (7)). It must be provided regularly for each model from first supply, visibly at sale and online, in an understandable language determined by the Member State. Distributors must display it and explain free return; commercial confidentiality must be preserved.

From 18 February 2027, the QR code on a battery outside mandatory passport scope must give access to the applicable Article 74(1)(a)–(f) information (Article 13(6)(b)). Passport batteries follow Article 77 and Annex XIII. See Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

16.4 Handover and transaction records ​

End-of-life vehicle and WEEE treatment-facility operators must hand removed batteries to the relevant producer, producer responsibility organisation or selected waste operator for Article 70 treatment and keep transaction records (Article 65(1) and (2)). Article 65 prescribes neither fields nor retention.

An operational record may associate date, source, destination, parties, category, chemistry, measured quantity, logistic-unit reference and intended operation. These are implementation data, not invented legal requirements. An implementation may use GLN for a party, function or location and SSCC for a pallet or container. GLN identifies the selected party, function or location; SSCC identifies the logistic unit. Neither replaces a permit, registration or official identifier, and an SSCC does not identify each battery inside it (GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.3–1.3.5 and 4.3; GS1 GLN Allocation Rules Standard, section 2.1).

16.5 Storage and transport ​

Collection infrastructure must suit expected volume and hazardous nature and meet applicable safety rules (Article 60(4)(a); Article 61(3)(a)). Portable and LMT transport containers must meet Directive 2008/68/EC requirements (Article 59(1)(c); Article 60(1)(c)). At treatment facilities, impermeable surfaces and weatherproof covering or suitable containers are required; batteries must not be mixed with conductive or combustible waste (Annex XII, Part A, points 2 and 3). Lithium-based batteries require the heat, water, damage, orientation, ventilation, isolation and warning controls in Annex XII, Part A, point 4.

Producers must give relevant operators safety information and, from first supply, provide relevant waste operators electronically, free and on request, with model-specific dismantling, storage, transport and treatment information, including components, materials and hazardous-substance locations (Article 74(2) and (3)). GS1 identifiers can link to controlled information; they do not determine dangerous-goods classification or transport compliance.

16.6 Treatment requirements ​

Separately collected batteries must undergo treatment and must not be disposed of or sent to energy recovery (Article 70(1)–(3); Annex XII, Part A, points 1, 5 and 6). Treatment must occur in a permitted facility, comply with Annex XII, Part A and use best available techniques under Directive 2010/75/EU. It must remove fluids and acids; mercury and cadmium must each be separated into identifiable streams. Batteries must be removed from WEEE, end-of-life vehicles and other waste products during treatment.

The Regulation prescribes no GS1 standard. An implementation may use GS1 identification structures for batteries. GTIN identifies a battery model or trade item; GTIN plus batch or lot number identifies a batch; GTIN plus serial number identifies an instance. A GTIN alone does not identify an individual, and no identifier replaces testing, sorting or operator duties (GS1 Global Traceability Standard, sections 4.1.1 and 5.2).

16.7 Recycling efficiency and material recovery ​

Recyclers must accept all waste batteries made available for preparation or recycling, subject to applicable Union law (Article 71(1)). Targets are (Article 71(2) and (3); Annex XII, Parts B and C):

MeasureTarget
Efficiency by 31 December 2025Lead-acid 75%; lithium-based 65%; nickel-cadmium 80%; other 50% by average weight
Efficiency by 31 December 2030Lead-acid 80%; lithium-based 70% by average weight
Recovery by 31 December 2027Cobalt, copper, lead and nickel 90% each; lithium 50%
Recovery by 31 December 2031Cobalt, copper, lead and nickel 95% each; lithium 80%

Article 71(4) requires calculation, verification and documentation rules. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 is listed as a source gap but is not stored locally. Calculation boundaries, allocation, evidence and templates must be verified against the authentic act.

Reporting covers every recycling step and output fraction; in multi-facility recycling, the first recycler collects and reports the information (Article 75(5)). Reports are due within six months; the first period is the first full calendar year after entry into force of the Article 76(5) act (Article 75(7)). Authorities establish electronic systems and formats (Article 75(8) and (9)). Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289 is a source gap; its authentic text is needed to confirm the first reporting year and formats. GS1 identifiers may support source records, but authority classifications govern statutory output.

16.9 How GS1 identifiers may support waste-stream records ​

The Regulation does not prescribe GS1 identifiers for waste-stream records.

Requirement or operational needGS1 applicationExplanation
Identify the battery model or trade item represented in a waste-stream recordGTIN identifies the battery model or battery managed as a trade item. Associated data records the applicable battery category, chemistry and other required attributes.A GTIN is not an individual battery identifier and does not itself communicate category, chemistry, condition or measured quantity. Where a battery is incorporated in another product, the battery identifier must remain distinguishable from the host-product identifier.
Distinguish a production batch where waste-stream evidence remains available at batch levelGTIN plus batch/lot number identifies the relevant battery batch/lot.Batch/lot identification may support sorting, investigation and reconciliation, but it is insufficient where evidence must concern an individual battery. Chapter VIII does not require every waste battery to retain batch-level identity.
Preserve individual battery identity where it remains available and is needed by the implementationGTIN plus serial number identifies an individual battery instance.Chapter VIII does not impose universal serialisation of waste batteries. An unreadable or unavailable serial number must not be reconstructed or inferred, and a GTIN alone must not be treated as instance-level identification.
Identify parties, functions and locations involved in collection, handover, storage, treatment or reportingGLN identifies the relevant legal entity, organisational function or physical location. Separate GLNs can distinguish parties from operational sites.Article 65(2) requires specified treatment-facility operators to keep transaction records, but does not prescribe GLN or define the record fields. A GLN does not replace a permit, registration number or other authority-issued identifier.
Identify a pallet, cage, drum, container or other logistic unit used in the waste streamSSCC identifies the logistic unit. Governed records associate it with its measured or declared contents and preserve aggregation and separation relationships.An SSCC does not identify each battery inside the logistic unit or establish its contents. Mixed streams may support only logistic-unit or consignment identification, and measured quantities must not be inferred from the identifier.

Implementers select the needed granularity, preserve aggregation and separation associations, and record measured quantities rather than infer them from identifiers. Mixed bulk streams may support only consignment or logistic-unit identification; unavailable instance identity must not be invented (GS1 Global Traceability Standard, sections 4.1 and 5.2–5.4).

16.10 How EPCIS may support collection and treatment events ​

The Regulation does not prescribe EPCIS. An implementation may use EPCIS for events concerning identified batteries, batches, logistic units, parties and locations. EPCIS captures those events (GS1 EPCIS Standard, version 2.0.1, sections 7.2.2 and 7.4.1–7.4.6). ObjectEvent records observations or status changes; AggregationEvent associates objects with a parent logistic unit; TransformationEvent expresses recycling inputs and outputs (sections 7.4.2, 7.4.3 and 7.4.5). CBV terms such as collecting, decommissioning, disassembling and inspecting must follow their complete definitions and context (GS1 Core Business Vocabulary Standard, sections 7.1–7.4).

The design must define event ownership, granularity, aggregation, measurement, corrections, access, confidentiality, retention and reconciliation with Article 75 aggregates. EPCIS can support evidence management; it does not prove permitting, correct treatment or target achievement and does not replace statutory reports, verification, audits or authority decisions.

16.11 Mixed and damaged battery streams ​

Collection covers batteries regardless of condition, while infrastructure and frequency must reflect volume and hazardous nature (Articles 59(1), 60(1) and (4), and 61(1) and (3)). Annex XII adds lithium precautions and prohibits mixing with conductive or combustible waste (Annex XII, Part A, points 3 and 4).

A mixed container may be identified by SSCC and associated with measured or estimated content by category and chemistry. After sorting, new logistic units and measured contents can be related to the incoming unit. Damaged, leaking or thermally affected batteries must be segregated and recorded at the level needed for safety and transport controls. If an original label, serial number, QR code or passport identifier is unreadable, an operator must not create an unverified link. A local consignment identifier may preserve custody while recording that original identity is unknown.

16.12 Limitations and unresolved questions ​

  • The status of acts amending Article 59 or 60 methods or targets is unverified.
  • Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 must be reviewed before recycling calculations are finalised.
  • Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289 must be reviewed before the first Article 75 period and formats are finalised.
  • Article 65(2) prescribes neither record content nor retention; national rules may add requirements.
  • Chapter VIII does not require every waste battery to remain individually identifiable.
  • GS1 identifiers and EPCIS are options, not legal requirements, permits or proof of compliance.
  • Article 74 information needs governed access while Article 74(7) confidentiality is preserved.

These matters are recorded in research/open-questions.md and research/source-gaps.md. Until the missing acts are controlled sources, calculation and reporting details are not final implementation instructions.
